The 120 arpeggio exercises are the first part of Op. 1 by Mauro Giuliani (1781–1829), Studio per la chitarra, published in Vienna around 1812. They all share the simplest possible harmonic scheme: one bar over a C major chord, another over G with a seventh (with the B in the bass), a repeat and a final C chord. What changes from one exercise to the next is the combination of right-hand fingers (p, i, m, a) and the order in which the strings are plucked, so the left hand hardly moves and the attention stays on the right.

The notes and rhythms come from the transcription by Marieh (2020), published on IMSLP and dedicated to the public domain (Creative Commons Zero 1.0).

The tablature was generated by algorithms. Although we review it by hand, you may find a mistake. It may differ from Marieh’s fingering. We keep the left-hand fingers (numbers) and right-hand fingers (p i m a) from that edition, which writes them only in the first bar of each exercise.
